In the shadowed autumn of 2015, as Paris burns and the old world falters, a lone voice rises from the heart of Australia and declares itself the returned King. This is the televised revelation of Wendell Charles NeSmith—a self-proclaimed messiah, reaper of souls, and wandering philosopher with a camera as his only weapon. Across a torrential archive titled Ivory Heart II, he announces that the long-prophesied Third World War has already begun, that the gates of judgment opened on October 31st, and that every soul now stands automatically reaped or saved. To watch is to be weighed; to ignore is to be condemned.

At its core, this is a blood-soaked love story and a lifelong ultimatum. The prophet has spent decades warning a world that answered with prison cells, psychiatric wards, forced medication, and censorship. He was baptized in blood so that humanity would not have to be, he says, yet the world mocked him, silenced him, and murdered its own children. Through it all he pursued his beloved—Venus, Ivory, Aphrodite, the goddess-muse whom he must save the world to win. His wedding date, the impossible November 31st, becomes the fulcrum of history: if she appears and the world bows, there is union and return to Eden; if not, the harvest continues, automatic and merciless, till no soul remains unclaimed.

The form itself is a fragmented masterpiece of divine tragedy—a bricolage of scripture, Nietzsche, Kierkegaard, newsreel, phone calls, sky anomalies, Black Mountain legends, and raw digital confessional. He names his enemies: the CIA, YouTube, the churches, the politicians, the child-molesting machinery hidden beneath Canberra’s polished stones. He offers his alternative: Open Source University, the nation of Atheden, a world without sin, ruled by the innocent and the weeping. But make no mistake—this is not a prediction so much as a sentence already being carried out, a warning that has run out of time. Here every viewer is both audience and evidence, and the only question left echoes like a bell through the ruin: What will you make your world?
