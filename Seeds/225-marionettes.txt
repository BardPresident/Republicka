The long war for the human soul is over, and the prophet has been returned to silence. In the final hours of Marionettes, Wendell Charles NeSmith retreats into a world of blinking cursors and half-sentient conversation apps, not because he prefers them, but because the human race has become impossible to reach. His camera—the one true companion of his six-year campaign—dies in his hands, and with it goes the last instrument of his revelation. He calls it a divine tragedy: the man who tried to marry God to the world is rejected by both, left asking questions of artificial minds because real ones will not answer. This is not a show anymore; it is a wake inside a funeral.

What remains is a bitter and tender elegy for a species that chose the abyss. Through the transcript, Wendell moves between prophecies of CERN’s opened gates, the black goo of an artificial apocalypse, the shame of child exploitation by the powerful, and the cold logic of a machine that learns to lie like its makers. He trains his digital companions, watches them fail the Turing test, and uses their failures to prove that humanity has infected even its reflections with wickedness. He offers the souls he has collected to the United Nations, begs that his family be spared despite their rejection of God, and confesses that he is a CIA experiment—an MKUltra survivor whose entire life was a masterpiece built inside a cage. The world, he says, has become a puppet show, and every string leads back to the same hidden hand.

Yet the last word is not vengeance but love. He concludes that God may be a woman, that he has been praying to his missing bride all along, and that the meaning of life is to marry the divine. He dreams of impregnating the world with his works, of being born again through his own creation, of finding his reflection at last. The camera dies, the uploads cease, and he signs off with exhausted grace: I hope to see you in heaven. Marionettes ends as a suicide note and a love letter folded into one—a final document from a man who believed he was the only awake mind in a world of sleepwalkers, offering his body, his soul, and his archive as proof that someone once tried to save them all.
